# Findings — 2026-07-28

**Run date:** 2026-07-28 · **Record:** Heraklion, PVGIS 5.3 / SARAH3, 2005–2023
· **Serves:** `FIX-1`, with a `CHORE-1` by-product · **Design:** [`PLAN.md`](PLAN.md)

**Result in one paragraph.** Against the pooled window it was actually fitted to, the
generator **does not overshoot at any of four dates**: at the shipped `bandwidth=0.1` the
mean daily total covers the observed mean everywhere (ratios 0.994–1.007, none excluding 1).
`FIX-1`'s headline — generated irradiance systematically too high — **is not reproduced**.
Two real defects are. First, **the overcast tail is missing**: where the record holds days
below 1 kWh/m², the model produces 0.2 % against 1.4 % observed at 21 March and 12.7 %
against 15.1 % at 21 December. Second, **the intended new default is worse than the one it
would replace**: Scott's rule pulls the mean *down* by 1.3–5.6 % at all four dates and
narrows the distribution.

## What was run

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Record | Heraklion (35.34, 25.14), 2005–2023 = **19 years**, 166 536 hourly rows |
| Attribute | `poa_global` |
| Window | **±7 days** — 285 pooled days per configuration (the shipped default is 15) |
| Bandwidth | **factor**: Scott's rule and 0.1 |
| Dates | 21 Mar · 21 Jun · 21 Sep · 21 Dec |
| Realisations | **n = 1000** per configuration, independent `gen_day` draws |
| Reference | **the pooled window itself** — known rather than estimated |
| Anchor | one extra cell at ±15 / 0.1, 21 Mar |
| Cost | 9 configurations, **16.6 min**; all 14 self-checks passing |

Every configuration compares two samples of daily totals: the **285 observed days** in that
date's window, and **1000 synthetic days** drawn from the generator fitted to those same
285. Because the model was fitted to exactly that pool, the pool is a known target rather
than an estimate of one — so only the synthetic side is resampled for intervals, and there
is no choice of reference to defend.

## Result 1 — no overshoot, at any date, at the shipped bandwidth

Mean of the daily total, synthetic / observed:

| date | bandwidth 0.1 | 95 % CI | Scott | 95 % CI |
| :--- | --: | :--- | --: | :--- |
| 21 Mar | 1.007 | [0.990, 1.025] | **0.967** | [0.950, 0.983] |
| 21 Jun | 0.998 | [0.992, 1.003] | **0.987** | [0.982, 0.992] |
| 21 Sep | 0.994 | [0.982, 1.006] | **0.978** | [0.967, 0.988] |
| 21 Dec | 1.001 | [0.978, 1.025] | **0.944** | [0.924, 0.964] |

At `0.1`, **0 of 4** dates exclude 1; the anchor cell at ±15 agrees at 1.001. The largest
deviation anywhere at the shipped setting is 0.7 %, against an interval half a percent wide
at 21 June — the sharpest cell in the design.

**This is not a null result from lack of power.** The positive control recovers an injected
+10 % as a lift of **1.100**, detected in **9 of 9** configurations. The instrument sees a
10 % effect everywhere; there is no 10 % effect to see.

## Result 2 — the overcast tail is missing

Only two dates can test this: 21 June and 21 September hold **no observed days** below
either threshold, so a deficit is not measurable there and those cells are excluded rather
than counted as agreement.

| date · threshold | observed | synthetic, Scott | synthetic, 0.1 |
| :--- | --: | --: | --: |
| 21 Mar · < 1 kWh/m² | 1.40 % | **0.20 %** | **0.20 %** |
| 21 Mar · < 0.25× median | 3.16 % | **1.00 %** | **1.10 %** |
| 21 Dec · < 1 kWh/m² | 15.09 % | **10.80 %** | **12.70 %** |
| 21 Dec · < 0.25× median | 1.75 % | **0.50 %** | **0.60 %** |

**2/2 informative dates, both thresholds, both bandwidths, every interval excluding.** The
deficit is ~7× at 21 March and ~1.2–1.4× at 21 December on the absolute threshold, and ~3×
at both dates on the relative one — so the *relative* deficit is roughly constant while the
absolute one is not, which is what a threshold that scales with the season should do.

**Mechanism:** a symmetric kernel across a bounded, right-skewed conditional density cannot
put enough mass near the floor without putting some below it.

**A deficit of the darkest days is not what raises a mean.** The two are measured in the
same cells and do not go together: the tail is missing at 21 March and 21 December, and the
mean is right at both.

## Result 3 — Scott's rule is the wrong default, and in a legible way

Every Scott cell is *below* 1 on the mean, and all four exclude:

| statistic | Scott, range over the four dates | dates excluding |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| mean of daily sum | 0.944 – 0.987 | **4/4 · consistent** |
| P50 of daily sum | 0.890 – 0.979 | **4/4 · consistent** |
| P90 of daily sum | 0.902 – 0.996 | **4/4 · consistent** |
| sd of daily sum | 0.796 – 0.922 | 3/4 |
| P10 of daily sum | 0.998 – 1.160 | 2/4 |

The signature is over-smoothing, not a shift: P10 goes **up** while P50 and P90 go **down**,
so the distribution is squeezed toward its centre and the mean falls with the long upper
tail. That is what a 3.9× wider kernel does — Scott chooses a factor of **0.390** at this
pool size against the hand-set **0.100**, and the two are the same kind of number, a
multiplier on the data covariance.

## Result 4 — mild under-dispersion, at both bandwidths

sd of the daily total is **0.891–1.001** at bandwidth 0.1 (2/4 dates excluding) and
0.796–0.922 at Scott (3/4). Under this design's framing that is a real defect: the model was
fitted to the pool it is compared against, so it should reproduce that pool's spread.

The daily **maximum** behaves differently. Its sd is *below* 1 at March, September and
December but **above** it at June (1.187 at 0.1, 1.345 at Scott). June's observed maxima are
nearly degenerate — sd 32.7 W/m² on a mean of 1006, because every June day is clear — and
the KDE cannot represent a spike that narrow.

## Result 5 — `CHORE-1`, at both window widths

Share of the pooled window's variance explained by position within the window — the cost of
pooling a window rather than a date:

| date | ±7 | ±15 |
| :--- | --: | --: |
| 21 Mar | **3.8 %** | **10.4 %** |
| 21 Jun | 1.0 % | — |
| 21 Sep | **19.3 %** | — |
| 21 Dec | 0.4 % | — |

**Halving the window worked as predicted at 21 March** — 10.4 % → 3.8 %, close to the
fourfold reduction a locally linear trend implies. But **21 September is 19.3 % at ±7**,
worse than 21 March at twice the width. The seasonal slope is comparable at the two
equinoxes; what differs is the noise it is measured against — September's day-to-day CV is
19.9 % against March's 31.8 %, so the same seasonal signal is a much larger share of a
smaller variance.

**`CHORE-1` cannot be answered with one number.** The cost of a window depends on the date,
and it is worst where the record is *least* variable — the opposite of where intuition puts
it.

## Observed day-to-day variability, for sizing future runs

| date | CV of daily totals, ±7-day pool |
| :--- | --: |
| 21 Mar | 31.8 % |
| 21 Jun | **9.0 %** |
| 21 Sep | 19.9 % |
| 21 Dec | **41.4 %** |

21 June is the sharpest date to measure on and 21 December the noisiest — a factor of
twenty-one in variance between them. Any future run should size `n` from the date it
targets rather than from a single global number.

## Controls, and what they establish

| control | result |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Null** — observed pool resampled in place of the synthetic sample | 88/90 statistics cover the observed value. The two that do not are 2.2 % of the family, against the 5 % a 95 % interval permits. |
| **Positive** — synthetic sample inflated 10 % | lift recovered as **1.100**, detected in **9/9**. |
| **Anchor** — the ±15 / 0.1 configuration of the earlier pass | mean ratio 1.001 (earlier: 1.011); sd 0.962 (0.915); tail share 0.40 % (0.60 %); seasonal share **10.4 %** (10.4 %). |

The anchor is the load-bearing one. An earlier pass the same day measured that ±15 / 0.1
cell with a different harness — three references, a bootstrap over both samples, a sweep
across training-set sizes — and this run reproduces its numbers within Monte-Carlo error.
That design was retired for the simpler one `PLAN.md` describes; its figures survive here
only as the values the anchor is checked against (`config.ANCHOR_EXPECTED`).

Two things that earlier pass established which this run did not re-test: the per-hour bias
is **flat** through the day, so the compounding `FIX-1` describes does not appear; and the
result is **independent of record length** across k ∈ {19, 9, 3, 2} years, with two-year
cells at −0.02 % — which is what first put `FIX-1`'s two-year Paris measurement in doubt.

## Two harness corrections made during the run

Recorded because both would have produced confidently wrong readings, and neither was
caught by a test:

1. **A bootstrap of zero events returns `[0, 0]`.** When the model produces no day below a
   threshold, resampling it produces no day below the threshold every time, and the
   degenerate interval "excludes" any observed share however small the sample. The upper
   bound now falls back to the rule of three (`3/n`) — 0.3 % at n = 1000, still well below
   the observed shares, so the finding stands, but on an interval that means something.
2. **An empty observed tail is not evidence.** June and September hold no days below either
   threshold, so those cells were being counted as "did not confirm the deficit", diluting a
   2/2 result into a 2/4 one. Informative cells are now identified explicitly, and the
   consistency rule additionally requires the dates to **agree in sign**.

## What this does not establish

- **One site, one variable, independent draws.** Chained series (`generate_series`) are not
  exercised, so nothing here speaks to drift along a multi-day walk.
- **Two dates carry the tail finding.** June and September cannot test it at all.
- **The window comparison is two points on one date.** ±7 against ±15 at 21 March is not an
  evaluation of the default.
- **Nothing about `poa_direct`, Paris, or pre-0.2.0 code.** Out of scope by design.

## What follows

1. **`FIX-1`'s headline does not survive.** No overshoot at the shipped bandwidth at any of
   four dates, on a 19-year record, with an instrument demonstrated to detect 10 %. The
   +14 % belongs in a provenance note, not cited as a live figure.
2. **Restate it as the overcast deficit** — measured, reproduced at two dates and two
   thresholds, with a mechanism and an existing set of candidate fixes that act on exactly
   it: reflection at zero, a log or square-root transform, beta kernels near the bound.
3. **Do not adopt Scott's rule as the irradiance default.** It is worse than 0.1 on the mean
   and the spread at every date tested. `PERF-3` — that only Scott pickles — needs the
   custom `__reduce__` it already proposes.
4. **`CHORE-1` gets numbers, not an answer**: 3.8 % at ±7 and 10.4 % at ±15 on 21 March,
   plus the finding that the cost is date-dependent and worst at 21 September.
